# Visser on GLP-1 and Eli Lilly

## Executive Summary
- Jordi Visser repeatedly frames Eli Lilly as an "AI trade," not just a pharma or GLP-1 story.
- His core thesis is that GLP-1 cash flows are funding a larger strategic buildout: AI infrastructure, specialized models, partnerships, biotech acquisitions, and proprietary health data assets.
- He treats GLP-1s as an underappreciated application-layer winner in the AI trade, with second-order effects across obesity, chronic disease, addiction, longevity, healthcare costs, and even adjacent industries.
- He also argues the next leg of upside may come from Lilly using GLP-1 scale, patient data, and AI-enabled biology to expand into adjacent therapies such as cardiovascular / cholesterol treatments and longer-term longevity themes.

## Eli Lilly Thesis
- Visser repeatedly says Lilly should be viewed as an AI company, not merely a drug company.
- He emphasizes three pillars:
  - GLP-1 demand and growth.
  - AI infrastructure and partnerships.
  - Capital allocation into biotech IP and adjacent disease categories.
- On growth, he highlights revenue growth of roughly 55% year over year and says that is extraordinary for a trillion-dollar company.
- On products, he says Lilly has a dominant GLP-1 franchise with demand still ahead.
- On AI, he points to:
  - LillyPad / Lilly Pod
  - Tune Lab
  - Co-innovation Lab
  - Nvidia connection
  - Insilico Medicine partnership
  - Isomorphic Labs / DeepMind partnership
- On data, he argues the real-world information gathered from GLP-1 users may be one of Lilly's most valuable long-term assets because it can feed AI systems and support discovery in adjacent conditions.
- On M&A and IP strategy, he says Lilly is "buying up companies left and right," rolling up failed or underexploited biotech ideas and pairing them with AI.
- On pricing, he argues lower prices could expand demand rather than hurt the business, explicitly invoking Jevons paradox.
- On macro and policy, he suggests governments have an incentive to support therapies that reduce chronic disease burden, improve longevity, and ease entitlement pressures.
- His strongest framing is that GLP-1 cash flow is financing a specialized healthcare AI platform with much broader optionality than the market may be pricing.

## GLP-1 Investment Themes

## AI + Biotech / Pharma Connections
- Visser repeatedly argues biotech and healthcare are becoming a major application layer in the AI trade.
- He thinks the next important AI winners will include specialized model owners trained on proprietary health data, not just generic model vendors.
- LillyPad is his flagship example of this shift: a domain-specific AI factory trained on company-owned data rather than internet-scale public data.
- He repeatedly links AI to:
  - Drug discovery
  - Protein design
  - Gene editing
  - Rare disease investigation
  - Longevity research
  - Better diagnosis through richer personal data
- He sees failed or stalled biotech IP as newly valuable when combined with AI tools, because AI may help these ideas cross the "last mile" that older workflows could not.
- He also implies that pharma companies with cash, data, and internal compute may gain a durable edge over firms that only have one successful drug franchise.
